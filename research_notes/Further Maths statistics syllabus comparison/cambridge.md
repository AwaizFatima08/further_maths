## Q1. Every numbered syllabus point in Paper 4: confirm or correct the expected list

### Takeaway
The expected list is **confirmed**. Paper 4 has exactly five numbered sub-topics: 4.1 Continuous random variables; 4.2 Inference using normal and t-distributions; 4.3 χ²-tests; 4.4 Non-parametric tests; 4.5 Probability generating functions. The learning outcomes are **identical in the 2023–25, 2026–27 and 2028–30 syllabuses** (only line-wrapping differs), so there is no 2027 or 2028 content change.

### Cited Findings
- The syllabus overview lists Paper 4 Further Probability & Statistics as: 4.1 Continuous random variables, 4.2 Inference using normal and t-distributions, 4.3 χ²-tests, 4.4 Non-parametric tests, 4.5 Probability generating functions. — [9231 2026–27 syllabus, p.9](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- The section header states that knowledge of 9709 Papers 5 and 6 (Probability & Statistics) subject content is assumed for this component. — [9231 2026–27, p.27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

**4.1 Continuous random variables** (p.27). Candidates should be able to:
1. use a probability density function that may be defined piecewise;
2. use the general result E(g(X)) = ∫ f(x) g(x) dx, where f is the PDF of continuous random variable X and g(X) is a function of X;
3. understand and use the relationship between the PDF and the cumulative distribution function (CDF), and use either one to evaluate probabilities or percentiles;
4. use CDFs of related variables in simple cases. *Note/example:* given the CDF of X, find the CDF of a related variable Y, and hence its PDF, e.g. where Y = X³.
— [9231 2026–27, p.27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

**4.2 Inference using normal and t-distributions** (p.27). Candidates should be able to:
1. formulate hypotheses and apply a t-test about a population mean, using a small sample from a normal population of unknown variance;
2. calculate a pooled estimate of a population variance from two samples. *Note:* calculations may be based on raw or summarised data;
3. formulate hypotheses about the difference of population means and apply, as appropriate: a 2-sample t-test; a paired sample t-test; a test using a normal distribution. *Note:* candidates are expected to be able to select the test that fits the circumstances of the problem;
4. determine a confidence interval for a population mean, based on a small sample from a normal population with unknown variance, using a t-distribution;
5. determine a confidence interval for a difference of population means, using a t-distribution or a normal distribution as appropriate.
— [9231 2026–27, p.27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

**4.3 χ²-tests** (p.28). Candidates should be able to:
1. fit a theoretical distribution, as prescribed by a given hypothesis, to given data. *Note:* questions will not involve lengthy calculations;
2. use a χ²-test with the appropriate number of degrees of freedom to carry out the corresponding goodness-of-fit analysis. *Note:* classes should be combined so that each expected frequency is at least 5;
3. use a χ²-test with the appropriate number of degrees of freedom for independence in a contingency table. *Notes:* Yates' correction is not required; where appropriate, rows or columns should be combined so that each cell's expected frequency is at least 5.
— [9231 2026–27, p.28](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

**4.4 Non-parametric tests** (p.28). Candidates should be able to:
1. understand the idea of a non-parametric test and appreciate when one might be useful. *Example:* sampling from a population that cannot be assumed to be normally distributed;
2. understand the basis of the sign test, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. *Note:* this includes knowing that Wilcoxon tests are valid only for symmetrical distributions;
3. use a single-sample sign test and a single-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test to test a hypothesis about a population median. *Notes:* includes normal approximations where appropriate; no tied ranks, and no observations equal to the median value being tested;
4. use a paired-sample sign test, a Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test and a Wilcoxon rank-sum test, as appropriate, to test for identity of populations. *Notes:* includes normal approximations where appropriate; no tied ranks and no zero-difference pairs.
— [9231 2026–27, p.28](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

**4.5 Probability generating functions** (p.28). Candidates should be able to:
1. understand the concept of a PGF, and construct and use the PGF for given distributions. *Note:* includes the discrete uniform, binomial, geometric and Poisson distributions;
2. use formulae for the mean and variance of a discrete random variable in terms of its PGF, and use them to calculate the mean and variance of a given distribution;
3. use the result that the PGF of a sum of independent random variables is the product of their PGFs.
— [9231 2026–27, p.28](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

- **Stability across versions:** a line-by-line diff of the Paper 4 subject content shows the same bullets and notes in [2023–25](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/597381-2023-2025-syllabus.pdf), [2026–27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf) and [2028–30](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/744603-2028-2030-syllabus.pdf). The differences are only line breaks, page headers and a "continued" label.
- The 2028–30 syllabus says it has been updated but that there are no significant changes which affect teaching, and that textbooks endorsed for exams from 2020 are still suitable. — [9231 2028–30, "Changes to this syllabus"](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/744603-2028-2030-syllabus.pdf)
- The 2026–27 change log lists only typographical and weblink corrections (versions 2–5). — [9231 2026–27, p.49](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

### Inferences
- The user's expected list is complete and correct. A few items are easy to miss: piecewise PDFs; the E(g(X)) integral; percentiles from the CDF; the normal-distribution test for a difference of means (large samples, or known variances); and the CI for a difference of means. These are all explicit outcomes.
- Some things are **not** in Paper 4: Yates' correction (explicitly excluded); tied ranks and zero differences (explicitly excluded); moment generating functions; Spearman/PMCC correlation; regression; the F-test; and the χ²-test for a variance. None of them appears anywhere in the Paper 4 content.
- The CDF of a function Y = g(X) is limited to "simple cases". The official example is Y = X³, i.e. monotonic transformations.

### Gaps
- There is no formal "2027 update" separate from the 2026–27 syllabus. The 2026–27 document covers 2027, and the next syllabus (2028–30) has unchanged Paper 4 content.

## Q2. Exact wording on specific points (pooled variance, goodness-of-fit distributions, normal approximations for Wilcoxon)

### Takeaway
- **Pooled variance:** "calculate a pooled estimate of a population variance from two samples", from raw or summarised data. MF19 gives the formula s² = [Σ(x₁−x̄₁)² + Σ(x₂−x̄₂)²]/(n₁+n₂−2).
- **Goodness of fit:** the syllabus does **not list** which distributions may be fitted. It says only "a theoretical distribution, as prescribed by a given hypothesis". In practice these include the 9709 distributions and given PDFs.
- **Wilcoxon normal approximations:** these are required "where appropriate" for both single-sample and paired/two-sample uses. MF19 gives their means and variances.

### Cited Findings
- Pooled variance learning outcome (quoted): "calculate a pooled estimate of a population variance from two samples". The accompanying note says calculations may use raw or summarised data. — [9231 2026–27, p.27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- MF19, *Further Probability & Statistics, Sampling and testing*, "Two-sample estimate of a common variance": s² = [Σ(x₁ − x̄₁)² + Σ(x₂ − x̄₂)²] / (n₁ + n₂ − 2). — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.39](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- Goodness of fit is worded as fitting a theoretical distribution prescribed by a given hypothesis to given data, with no named list. The notes say questions will not involve lengthy calculations, and that classes should be combined so each expected frequency is ≥ 5. — [9231 2026–27, p.28](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- The prior-learning guide gives the Poisson distribution as an example of a distribution that "may be used as the theoretical distribution in a goodness-of-fit test, or in a probability generating function". It also says questions solely about the Poisson distribution will not be set. — [Guide to prior learning, p.3](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/676396-prior-learning-guidance.pdf)
- Examiner report evidence that continuous or given distributions are also used: in June 2023 9231/42 Q3, candidates found constants a and b of a given distribution and then carried out a goodness-of-fit test. Combining the first two columns was necessary because an expected frequency was below 5. — [9231 June 2023 Principal Examiner Report](https://pastpapers.co/cie/A-Level/Mathematics-Further-9231/2023-May-June/9231_s23_er.pdf)
- Wilcoxon and sign-test normal approximations are written into the notes as "Including the use of normal approximations where appropriate". This note is attached to both the single-sample bullet (sign test and Wilcoxon signed-rank) and the paired/identity-of-populations bullet (paired sign, matched-pairs signed-rank, rank-sum). — [9231 2026–27, p.28](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- MF19 **Wilcoxon signed-rank** table:
  - **Definitions:** P = sum of ranks of positive differences; Q = sum of ranks of negative differences; T = the smaller of P and Q.
  - **Critical values:** the table gives the largest T leading to rejection, for n = 6 to 20, at one-tailed 0.05/0.025/0.01/0.005 (two-tailed 0.1/0.05/0.02/0.01).
  - **Larger n:** P and Q are each approximated by a normal distribution with mean n(n+1)/4 and variance n(n+1)(2n+1)/24.
  — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.43](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- MF19 **Wilcoxon rank-sum** table:
  - **Definitions:** samples of sizes m ≤ n; R_m = sum of the ranks of the size-m sample; W = the smaller of R_m and m(n+m+1) − R_m.
  - **Critical values:** the table covers m = 3 to 10 and n = m to 10, at one-tailed 0.05/0.025/0.01 (two-tailed 0.1/0.05/0.02).
  - **Larger m and n:** R_m is approximated by a normal distribution with mean m(m+n+1)/2 and variance mn(m+n+1)/12.
  — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.44](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- Note on the symmetry assumption: the syllabus requires knowing that Wilcoxon tests are valid only for symmetrical distributions. — [9231 2026–27, p.28](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- 4.2 note on test choice: candidates are expected to choose between the 2-sample t-test, the paired t-test and the normal test from the circumstances of the problem. — [9231 2026–27, p.27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- PGF distributions named: discrete uniform, binomial, geometric and Poisson. — [9231 2026–27, p.28](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)

### Inferences
- The sign test has no MF19 table. Its approximation would be binomial B(n, ½) → normal, which is assumed knowledge from 9709 S1/S2 and is not printed in MF19.
- The pooled-variance formula appears only as the "raw" deviation form. Candidates must convert summary statistics (Σx, Σx²) themselves.
- Two-sample t-tests require the assumption of equal population variances, i.e. pooling. When variances are not assumed equal and the samples are large, the "test using a normal distribution" applies. The June 2023 report penalises pooling in that case (see Q6 below).

### Gaps
- No official list of goodness-of-fit distributions exists. The Cambridge wording is open-ended. Saying that binomial, Poisson, geometric, uniform, normal and given-PDF fits are all possible is an inference from the prior-knowledge scope and past-paper evidence, not an official statement.
- The syllabus does not state a threshold n at which the Wilcoxon normal approximation should be used. MF19 implies it is used beyond the tabulated range (n > 20 for signed-rank; m or n > 10 for rank-sum).

## Q3. Formulae and statistical tables in MF19 relevant to Paper 4

### Takeaway
MF19 is a combined 9709 and 9231 list, and all of it may be needed. Paper 4 candidates get the following:
- **Formulae:**
  - from the 9709 "Probability & Statistics" block: summary statistics, discrete RVs, binomial, geometric and Poisson pmfs with means and variances, continuous RV E(X) and Var(X), unbiased estimators, the CLT, and the sample-proportion approximation;
  - from the "Further Probability & Statistics" block: only the two-sample common-variance estimate and the PGF formulae.
- **Tables:** five statistical tables: the normal distribution function, normal critical values, t critical values, χ² critical values, and the Wilcoxon signed-rank and rank-sum critical values.

### Cited Findings
- Exam conditions: MF19 is supplied in exams. It is a combined formulae list for 9709 and 9231, and all formulae in it may be needed. — [9231 2026–27, p.30](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- MF19 **PROBABILITY & STATISTICS** block (9709 level) contains:
  - summary statistics: mean and standard deviation for ungrouped and grouped data;
  - discrete RVs: E(X) = Σxp and Var(X) = Σx²p − {E(X)}²;
  - Binomial B(n,p): p_r = C(n,r)p^r(1−p)^(n−r), μ = np, σ² = np(1−p);
  - Geometric Geo(p): p_r = p(1−p)^(r−1), μ = 1/p (**no variance given**);
  - Poisson Po(λ): p_r = e^(−λ)λ^r/r!, μ = λ, σ² = λ;
  - continuous RVs: E(X) = ∫x f(x)dx and Var(X) = ∫x² f(x)dx − {E(X)}²;
  - sampling and testing: unbiased estimators x̄ = Σx/n and s² = Σ(x−x̄)²/(n−1) = [Σx² − (Σx)²/n]/(n−1); the CLT X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n); and the sample proportion approximately N(p, p(1−p)/n).
  — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.38](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- MF19 **FURTHER PROBABILITY & STATISTICS** block contains only two items:
  - the two-sample estimate of a common variance (above);
  - PGFs: G_X(t) = E(t^X), E(X) = G′_X(1), and Var(X) = G″_X(1) + G′_X(1) − {G′_X(1)}².
  — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.39](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- **The normal distribution function table** gives Φ(z) = P(Z ≤ z) from 0 to just under 3 (rows 0.0–2.9 with differences columns). It is followed by **critical values for the normal distribution** at p = 0.75, 0.90, 0.95, 0.975, 0.99, 0.995, 0.9975, 0.999, 0.9995. — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.40](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- **Critical values for the t-distribution** give t with P(T ≤ t) = p, for p = 0.75, 0.90, 0.95, 0.975, 0.99, 0.995, 0.9975, 0.999, 0.9995. The degrees of freedom are ν = 1–30, then 40, 60, 120 and ∞. — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.41](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- **Critical values for the χ²-distribution** give x with P(X ≤ x) = p, for p = 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.9, 0.95, 0.975, 0.99, 0.995, 0.999. The degrees of freedom are ν = 1–25, then 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 and 100. — [9231 2026–27, MF19 p.42](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- **Wilcoxon signed-rank test** and **Wilcoxon rank-sum test** tables, with their normal approximations (see Q2). — [9231 2026–27, MF19 pp.43–44](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- The standalone MF19 PDF describes itself as the list for 9709 and 9231, for use from 2020 in all papers for those syllabuses. — [MF19 standalone](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/417318-list-of-formulae-and-statistical-tables.pdf) (taken from the search-result snippet; the content check was done on the syllabus copy)

### Inferences
These are **not given** in MF19, so candidates must recall them:
- the 2-sample t statistic itself, (x̄₁ − x̄₂)/√(s²(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)), and its degrees of freedom n₁ + n₂ − 2;
- the paired t statistic;
- the normal 2-sample statistic √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂);
- CI formulae;
- the χ² statistic Σ(O−E)²/E and the degrees-of-freedom rules ((r−1)(c−1); classes − 1 − number of estimated parameters);
- the PGFs of the standard distributions;
- the CDF definitions;
- the geometric variance (needed for PGF checks, although derivable via the PGF).

### Gaps
- The standalone MF19 PDF (417318) was not downloaded separately. Its content was taken from the copy printed in the 2026–27 syllabus, which the syllabus says is the version used in exams.

## Q5. Prior knowledge from 9709 S1/S2 that Paper 4 assumes

### Takeaway
The syllabus requires prior knowledge from **9709 Papers 1, 3, 5 and 6**: Pure 1, Pure 3 (calculus), Probability & Statistics 1 and 2. Cambridge's separate prior-learning guide divides 9709 Paper 6 (S2) content into three groups:
- **Required:** the Poisson basics, continuous RVs, sampling, CLT and unbiased estimates, and hypothesis-test terminology and Type I/II meaning;
- **Recommended:** linear combinations of normals, the CI for a mean with known variance or a large sample, and the z-test for a mean;
- **Not required:** the Poisson approximations, sampling methods, the proportion CI, binomial/Poisson single-observation tests, and Type I/II error calculations.

### Cited Findings
- Prior knowledge table: 9231 Paper 4 requires 9709 Papers 1, 3, 5 and 6. — [9231 2026–27, p.15](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf) (same in [2028–30](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/744603-2028-2030-syllabus.pdf))
- 9709 Paper 6 itself assumes Paper 5 knowledge, plus calculus within the Paper 3 content. — [9709 2026–27, S2 header](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697427-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)
- **9709 S1 (Paper 5) content** — [9709 2026–27, section 5](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697427-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf):
  - 5.1 Representation of data: stem-and-leaf (including back-to-back), box plots, histograms and cumulative frequency; mean, median and mode; range, IQR and s.d.; mean and s.d. from raw, grouped or coded totals.
  - 5.2 Permutations and combinations: arrangements with repetition and restriction, but no circular arrangements.
  - 5.3 Probability: enumeration; addition and multiplication; exclusive and independent events; conditional probability.
  - 5.4 Discrete random variables: probability distribution tables with E(X) and Var(X); binomial B(n,p) and geometric Geo(p) probabilities; binomial mean and variance; **geometric expectation only**.
  - 5.5 The normal distribution: standardising; finding μ and σ; the normal approximation to the binomial with continuity correction (np > 5 and nq > 5).
  - Probability notation assumed: P(A), P(A∪B), P(A∩B), P(A|B), and A′.
- **9709 S2 (Paper 6) content** — [9709 2026–27, section 6](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697427-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf):
  - 6.1 Poisson: Po(λ); mean = variance = λ; Poisson as a model; the Poisson approximation to the binomial (n > 50, np < 5); the normal approximation to the Poisson (λ > 15).
  - 6.2 Linear combinations: E/Var of aX + b and aX + bY; normal closure; Poisson sum.
  - 6.3 Continuous RVs: PDFs **over a single interval only** (the domain may be infinite); probabilities, mean, variance, median and percentiles by area. **Explicit knowledge of the CDF is not included.**
  - 6.4 Sampling and estimation: sample vs population; randomness; E(X̄) = μ and Var(X̄) = σ²/n; X̄ normal if X is normal; informal CLT; unbiased estimates of mean and variance; CI for a mean (known variance or large sample); approximate CI for a proportion.
  - 6.5 Hypothesis tests: terminology (null and alternative hypotheses, significance level, critical/rejection region, acceptance region, test statistic, one- and two-tailed); single-observation binomial and Poisson tests; z-test for a mean; Type I and II errors.
- The prior-learning guide's own framing:
  - ideally candidates cover all of 9709 Paper 6 before 9231 Paper 4;
  - the guide marks S2 content as required (not highlighted), recommended (yellow) or not required (grey);
  - required knowledge may be needed when answering questions but will not be the main focus, e.g. Poisson in goodness-of-fit or PGF questions;
  - the S2 confidence intervals and hypothesis tests will not be directly tested in 9231 Paper 4, but they form a basis for its intervals and tests.
  — [Guide to prior learning, p.3](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/676396-prior-learning-guidance.pdf)
- Guide classification of S2 outcomes, read from the rendered page highlighting — [Guide to prior learning, pp.4–6](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/676396-prior-learning-guidance.pdf):
  - **6.1:** Po(λ) probabilities, mean = variance = λ, and Poisson as a model are **required**. The Poisson approximation to the binomial and the normal approximation to the Poisson are **not required** (grey).
  - **6.2:** the E/Var results for aX + b and aX + bY, and normal closure of aX + b and aX + bY, are **recommended** (yellow). The "sum of independent Poissons is Poisson" item appears **grey (not required)**; this was read from a low-resolution render, so confidence is moderate.
  - **6.3:** both continuous-RV outcomes are **required**.
  - **6.4:** sample vs population, E(X̄) and Var(X̄), X̄ normal, CLT and unbiased estimates are **required**. Explaining why a sampling method is unsatisfactory is **not required** (grey). The CI for a mean (known variance or large sample) is **recommended** (yellow). The approximate CI for a proportion is **not required** (grey).
  - **6.5:** test terminology and the meaning of Type I/II errors are **required**. Single-observation binomial/Poisson tests are **not required** (grey). ## Q6 (bonus). Common errors from examiner reports

### Takeaway
The June 2023 Principal Examiner Report (9231/42 and /43; /41 had too few candidates for a report) names these recurring faults:
- conclusions stated without uncertainty, or tests "proving" things;
- hypotheses not stated in terms of the population parameter (e.g. "median" without "population", or using μ in Wilcoxon tests);
- wrong t or χ² table values, or z used instead of t;
- pooling variances when not justified, or failing to pool when told the variances are equal;
- incomplete CDFs, with "0 otherwise" given instead of F = 1 above the range;
- confusing the PDF with the CDF when finding a median or percentile;
- adding PGFs instead of multiplying them;
- premature rounding.

### Cited Findings
All findings below come from the [9231 June 2023 Principal Examiner Report](https://pastpapers.co/cie/A-Level/Mathematics-Further-9231/2023-May-June/9231_s23_er.pdf).
- **9231/41:** too few candidates for a meaningful report.
- **Key messages (both variants):** decide explicitly whether to reject H₀, then conclude in context with some uncertainty. A test is not a proof.
- **Rounding:** premature rounding caused 3 s.f. errors; work to at least 4 s.f.
- **/42 Q1 (CI):** wrong t-value (often 1.796); some used z = 1.645 or 1.96 for a small sample with unknown variance.
- **/42 Q2:** two-sample t-test with pooled variance. A minority ignored the stated assumption of equal population variances.
- **/42 Q3 (goodness of fit):** classes had to be combined when an expected frequency was below 5. Hypotheses were too vague; they should mention both the distribution and the data. Wrong tabular values were used.
- **/42 Q4 (Wilcoxon matched-pairs):** the correct critical value was 21 and the common wrong value was 17. Hypotheses should refer to the population median. Some used a paired t-test inappropriately.
- **/42 Q6 and /43 Q1 (CDF):**
  - the constant of integration was omitted;
  - F(x) = 0 below and F(x) = 1 above the range were merged as "0 otherwise";
  - after finding the CDF of Y, some did not differentiate it to get the PDF;
  - when solving the quadratic from the CDF, some kept the root outside the domain;
  - some set the PDF equal to the probability instead of the CDF.
- **/43 Q2 (CI from given endpoints):** the correct t was 1.771; adjacent table values and z were also seen.
- **/43 Q3(b):** very few candidates could state the Wilcoxon assumption, which is symmetry of the population of differences.
- **/43 Q4 (two-sample, large samples):** pooling was inappropriate because there was no equal-variance assumption, so separate variances and the normal test were used. Saying only "n > 30" was not enough to justify the CLT.
- **/43 Q5 (PGF):** "show that" steps were missing (G(1) = 1 to find k). Some added PGFs instead of multiplying. Some confused the mode with E(Z).
- **/43 Q6 (contingency table):** the correct critical value was 7.378. Some worded the conclusion wrongly ("insufficient evidence that … independent").

### Inferences
- The reports show that test selection is examined in practice: pooled vs unpooled, t vs z, and Wilcoxon vs t. This matches the 4.2 note on choosing the appropriate test.
