## Every coded spec point of the compulsory content, with AS/A-level status

### Takeaway
AQA codes the compulsory content in 10 lettered sections, A to J, numbered A1 to J3: 79 points in total, plus the overarching themes OT1 to OT3. The AS spec (7366) covers only sections A to H. It reuses the A-level codes for a subset of 41 points, leaving gaps in the numbering (e.g. D5 and D7 are missing). Ten of those shared points have narrower wording at AS. Sections I (Differential equations) and J (Numerical methods) are A-level only.

### Cited Findings
Structure:
- 7367: "The subject content in sections A to J is compulsory for all students. Students must study two of the optional applications": mechanics (MA–ME), statistics (SA–SH), discrete (DA–DG). — [7367 spec §3](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)
- 7366: "sections A to H is compulsory". Students study two options: mechanics (MA–MD), statistics (SA–SH), discrete (DA–DG). — [7366 spec §3](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)
- Overarching themes (both specs): OT1 Mathematical argument, language and proof (OT1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4 [A-level only; AS list jumps 1.3→1.5], 1.5); OT2 Mathematical problem solving (OT2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.6, 2.7); OT3 Mathematical modelling (OT3.1–3.5). The jumps in the numbering are in the original. — [7367](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF), [7366](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)

Point-by-point list. Wording follows the 7367 spec, closely paraphrased or verbatim. The status comes from comparing it with the 7366 spec. Key: **AS** = appears in 7366 with identical wording; **AS (narrower)** = appears in 7366 with reduced scope, AS wording given; **A-level only** = absent from 7366. Sources: [7367 §3.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF), [7366 §3.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF).

**A: Proof**
- A1: Construct proofs using mathematical induction; contexts include sums of series, divisibility, and powers of matrices. **AS**

**B: Complex numbers**
- B1: Solve any quadratic with real coefficients; solve cubic or quartic equations with real coefficients (given enough information to deduce at least one root for cubics, or at least one complex root or quadratic factor for quartics). **AS**
- B2: Add, subtract, multiply and divide complex numbers x + iy; 'real part' and 'imaginary part'. **AS**
- B3: Complex conjugate; non-real roots of real polynomials occur in conjugate pairs. **AS**
- B4: Use and interpret Argand diagrams. **AS**
- B5: Convert between Cartesian and modulus-argument form (radians assumed). **AS**
- B6: Multiply and divide in modulus-argument form (radians and compound angle formulae assumed). **AS**
- B7: Construct and interpret simple loci in the Argand diagram such as |z − a| > r and arg(z − a) = θ. **AS**
- B8: Understand de Moivre's theorem and use it to find multiple angle formulae and sums of series. **A-level only**
- B9: Know and use the definition e^{iθ} = cos θ + i sin θ and the form z = re^{iθ}. **A-level only**
- B10: Find the n distinct nth roots of re^{iθ} for r ≠ 0 and know they form the vertices of a regular n-gon. **A-level only**
- B11: Use complex roots of unity to solve geometric problems. **A-level only**

**C: Matrices**
- C1: Add, subtract and multiply conformable matrices; scalar multiplication. **AS**
- C2: Zero and identity matrices. **AS**
- C3: Matrices for linear transformations in 2D; successive transformations; single transformations in 3D (confined to reflection in x = 0, y = 0 or z = 0, or rotation about a coordinate axis). **AS**
- C4: Invariant points and lines for a linear transformation. **AS**
- C5: Determinants of 2×2 and 3×3 matrices, interpreted as scale factors, including the effect on orientation. **AS (narrower)**: AS says only "Calculate determinants of 2 × 2 matrices."
- C6: Singular and non-singular matrices; properties of inverse matrices; calculate and use the inverse of non-singular 2×2 and 3×3 matrices. **AS (narrower)**: 2×2 inverses only.
- C7: Solve three linear simultaneous equations in three variables using the inverse matrix. **A-level only**
- C8: Interpret geometrically the solution, and failure of solution, of three simultaneous linear equations. **A-level only**
- C9: Factorise determinants using row and column operations. **A-level only**
- C10: Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of 2×2 and 3×3 matrices; characteristic equation; their geometrical significance. **A-level only**
- C11: Diagonalisation, M = UDU⁻¹ and Mⁿ = UDⁿU⁻¹, when the eigenvalues are real. **A-level only**

**D: Further algebra and functions**
- D1: Relationship between roots and coefficients of polynomial equations up to quartics. **AS**
- D2: Form a polynomial equation whose roots are a linear transformation of the roots of a given polynomial (at least cubic). **AS**
- D3: Formulae for sums of integers, squares and cubes, and their use to sum other series. **AS**
- D4: Method of differences for summation of series, including use of partial fractions. **AS (narrower)**: the AS wording omits "including use of partial fractions".
- D5: Find the Maclaurin series of a function, including the general term. **A-level only**
- D6: Recognise and use the Maclaurin series for eˣ, ln(1 + x), sin x, cos x and (1 + x)ⁿ, and their ranges of validity (proof not required). **AS**
- D7: Evaluate limits using Maclaurin series or l'Hôpital's rule. **A-level only**
- D8: Inequalities involving polynomial equations (cubic and quartic). **AS**
- D9: Solve inequalities such as (ax + b)/(cx + d) < ex + f algebraically. **AS**
- D10: Modulus of functions and associated inequalities. **A-level only**
- D11: Graphs of y = |f(x)| and y = 1/f(x) for a given y = f(x). (The extracted text was garbled; the reading of the symbols is inferred.) **A-level only**
- D12: Graphs of (ax + b)/(cx + d); asymptotes, intersections with the axes or other lines; associated inequalities. **AS**
- D13: Graphs of (ax² + bx + c)/(dx² + ex + f), including cases where some coefficients are zero; asymptotes parallel to the axes; oblique asymptotes. **AS (narrower)**: the AS wording ends at "asymptotes parallel to coordinate axes" (no oblique asymptotes).
- D14: Use quadratic theory (not calculus) to find the possible values of the function, and the stationary points, for (ax² + bx + c)/(dx² + ex + f). **AS**
- D15: Sketch y² = 4ax, x²/a² + y²/b² = 1, x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 and xy = c², including intercepts and the asymptotes of hyperbolas. **AS**
- D16: Single transformations of curves (translations, stretches parallel to the axes, reflections in the axes and in y = ±x), extended to composite transformations including rotations and enlargements. **AS (narrower)**: single transformations only, with no composite, rotation or enlargement sentence.

**E: Further calculus**
- E1: Improper integrals, where the integrand is undefined at a value in the range or the range extends to infinity. **A-level only**
- E2: Derive formulae for, and calculate, volumes of revolution. **AS**
- E3: Understand and evaluate the mean value of a function. **AS**
- E4: Integrate using partial fractions, extended to quadratic factors (ax² + c) in the denominator. **A-level only**
- E5: Differentiate inverse trigonometric functions. **A-level only**
- E6: Integrate functions of the form (a² − x²)^(−1/2) and (a² + x²)^(−1), and choose trigonometric substitutions for associated functions. **A-level only**
- E7: Arc length and area of surface of revolution, for curves in Cartesian or parametric coordinates. **A-level only**
- E8: Derive and use reduction formulae for integration. **A-level only**
- E9: The limits lim_{x→∞} xᵏe^{−x} and lim_{x→0} xᵏ ln x (k > 0), applied to improper integrals. **A-level only**

**F: Further vectors**
- F1: Vector and Cartesian forms of the equation of a straight line in 3D. **AS**
- F2: Vector and Cartesian forms of the equation of a plane. **A-level only**
- F3: Scalar product, used for the angle between two lines, the equation of a plane, the angle between two planes, and the angle between a line and a plane. **AS (narrower)**: "Calculate the scalar product and use it to calculate the angle between two lines."
- F4: Check perpendicularity using the scalar product. **AS**
- F5: Vector product and its properties; the line (r − a) × b = 0; area of a triangle by vector product. **A-level only**
- F6: Intersection of two lines; intersection of a line and a plane; perpendicular distance between two lines, from a point to a line, and from a point to a plane. **AS (narrower)**: intersection of two lines, and perpendicular distance between two lines and from a point to a line (no planes).

**G: Polar coordinates**
- G1: Polar coordinates; convert between polar and Cartesian. **AS**
- G2: Sketch curves with r given as a function of θ, including trigonometric functions. **AS**
- G3: Find the area enclosed by a polar curve. **A-level only**

**H: Hyperbolic functions**
- H1: Definitions, domains and ranges of sinh, cosh and tanh, and sketching their graphs; definitions, domains and ranges of sech, cosech and coth. **AS (narrower)**: "Understand the definitions of hyperbolic functions sinh x, cosh x and tanh x, and be able to sketch their graphs." This covers no reciprocal functions, and domains/ranges are not mentioned.
- H2: Differentiate and integrate hyperbolic functions. **A-level only**
- H3: Definitions of the inverse hyperbolic functions, and their domains and ranges. **AS (narrower)**: definitions only; domains/ranges not mentioned.
- H4: Derive and use the logarithmic forms of the inverse hyperbolic functions. **AS**
- H5: Integrate (x² + a²)^(−1/2) and (x² − a²)^(−1/2), and choose substitutions for associated functions. **A-level only**
- H6: tanh x ≡ sinh x / cosh x; cosh²x − sinh²x ≡ 1; sech²x ≡ 1 − tanh²x; cosech²x ≡ coth²x − 1; cosh 2x ≡ cosh²x + sinh²x; sinh 2x ≡ 2 sinh x cosh x. **AS (narrower)**: tanh x ≡ sinh x/cosh x and cosh²x − sinh²x ≡ 1 only.
- H7: Construct proofs involving hyperbolic functions and identities. **A-level only**

**I: Differential equations** (whole section A-level only)
- I1: Integrating factor for dy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x); recognise when it is appropriate.
- I2: General and particular solutions of differential equations.
- I3: Differential equations in modelling, in kinematics and other contexts.
- I4: y″ + ay′ + by = 0 (constant a, b) via the auxiliary equation.
- I5: y″ + ay′ + by = f(x): complementary function plus particular integral, for f(x) polynomial, exponential or trigonometric.
- I6: How the discriminant of the auxiliary equation (positive, zero, negative) relates to the form of the solution.
- I7: Solve the SHM equation ẍ = −ω²x and relate the solution to the motion.
- I8: Model damped oscillations with 2nd-order DEs; light, critical and heavy damping, and when each occurs.
- I9: Coupled pairs of 1st-order simultaneous equations (one independent and two dependent variables), e.g. predator-prey models.
- I10: Hooke's law T = kx to formulate an SHM differential equation.
- I11: Models for damped motion, with the damping force proportional to velocity.

**J: Numerical methods** (whole section A-level only)
- J1: Mid-ordinate rule and Simpson's rule for integration.
- J2: Euler's step-by-step method for first-order differential equations.
- J3: Improved Euler method for first-order DEs: y_{r+1} = y_{r−1} + 2h f(x_r, y_r), x_{r+1} = x_r + h.

### Inferences
- Counts from the list above: A-level compulsory = A1, B1–B11, C1–C11, D1–D16, E1–E9, F1–F6, G1–G3, H1–H7, I1–I11, J1–J3, which is 79 points. The AS spec uses 41 of these codes, 10 of them narrower.
- AS-absent codes: B8–B11, C7–C11, D5, D7, D10, D11, E1, E4–E9, F2, F5, G3, H2, H5, H7, all of I, and all of J.
- AQA's AS content is a strict subset of the A-level compulsory content with the same codes. So for a co-teachable AS year, the AS points (and the AS halves of the narrower points) are effectively the "Year 1" content.
- Unlike AQA Mathematics, AQA Further Maths has no separate Paper 1 and Paper 2 content split: both A-level pure papers may assess all of A–J.

## Formulae in the AQA formulae booklet (Further Maths pure) vs what must be memorised (spec Appendix B)

### Takeaway
AQA uses one booklet, "Formulae and statistical tables" (v1.5, Feb 2018), for all AS and A-level Further Maths exams. Spec Appendix B separately lists the formulae students must know without being given them. Appendix B is identical in both specs and includes some FM-specific items: complex multiplication and division in exponential form, the Argand loci, the 2×2 determinant and inverse, (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹, roots-coefficient relations for quadratics and cubics, the exponential definitions of cosh, sinh and tanh, volumes of revolution, SHM, and the scalar product, line and plane equations.

### Inferences
- Notable pure formulae that are not in the booklet and not in Appendix B, so students must know or derive them: the quartic roots-coefficient relations (D1 covers up to quartics, but Appendix B lists only quadratic and cubic); sech²x ≡ 1 − tanh²x and cosech²x ≡ coth²x − 1 (H6, not in the booklet); the derivatives and integrals of sinh and cosh; the mean value formula (1/(b − a))∫f dx; the integrating-factor method; and the polar–Cartesian conversions x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ. These come from my comparison of the three documents, not from an AQA statement.
- A line-by-line comparison of the Appendix B headings and text in 7366 and 7367 found them identical: AS students get the same must-memorise list, including items such as e^{iθ} that go beyond AS content.

## Paper structure (A-level Papers 1–3; AS Papers 1–2)

### Takeaway
A-level 7367 has three 2-hour, 100-mark papers, each worth 33⅓%. Papers 1 and 2 are both entirely compulsory pure (sections A–J, either paper may test any of it), and Paper 3 covers the two chosen applied options. AS 7366 has two papers of 1 h 30 min and 80 marks, each worth 50%: Paper 1 is compulsory pure (A–H) and Paper 2 is the two options.

### Cited Findings
- 7367 Paper 1: "May assess content from" A: Proof, B: Complex numbers, C: Matrices, D: Further algebra and functions, E: Further calculus, F: Further vectors, G: Polar coordinates, H: Hyperbolic functions, I: Differential equations, J: Numerical methods. Written exam, 2 hours, 100 marks, 33⅓% of A-level. "A mix of question styles, from short, single-mark questions to multi-step problems." — [7367 §2.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)
- 7367 Paper 2: same section list (A–J), 2 hours, 100 marks, 33⅓%. — [7367 §2.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)
- 7367 Paper 3: 2 hours, 100 marks, 33⅓%. It is one of three combinations, each with two answer booklets: Discrete + Statistics, Statistics + Mechanics, or Mechanics + Discrete. — [7367 §2.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)
- 7366 Paper 1: May assess A–H (Proof, Complex numbers, Matrices, Further algebra and functions, Further calculus, Further vectors, Polar coordinates, Hyperbolic functions). Written exam, 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, 50% of AS. — [7366 §2.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)
- 7366 Paper 2: 1 h 30 min, 80 marks, 50% of AS. Its option pairs are the same three as the A-level: Discrete + Statistics, Statistics + Mechanics, or Mechanics + Discrete. — [7366 §2.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)
- Option section codes: A-level mechanics is MA–ME; AS mechanics is MA–MD. Statistics is SA–SH and discrete is DA–DG in both. — [7367 §3](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF), [7366 §3](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)
- Calculators (both specs): "A calculator is required for use in all assessments". The required minimum is an iterative function, matrix calculations up to order 3×3, and summary statistics and probabilities from standard distributions. — [7367 §5.9](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF), [7366 §5.9](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)
- First exams: AS June 2018 onwards; A-level June 2019 onwards. — [7366 footer](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF), [7367 footer](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)

### Inferences
- Compulsory pure makes up 200 of 300 marks (⅔) of the A-level and 80 of 160 marks (50%) of the AS. Pure content is not split between Papers 1 and 2.
- There is no "pure option" route: Paper 3 is always two applied options, so every candidate studies two of mechanics, statistics and discrete.

- The spec gives no breakdown of marks within Paper 3 (e.g. 50/50 per option booklet). It is not stated in the spec text I read; past papers would confirm it.
