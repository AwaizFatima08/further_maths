# Forty units map every Further Pure syllabus

The six Further Mathematics specifications share a large common pure core, but they split it into AS and A level in different places, and that is where a single workbook gets hard. **Proof by induction, complex numbers up to de Moivre and roots of unity, roots of polynomials, series summation, matrices up to 3×3 inverses, lines and planes, Maclaurin series, hyperbolic functions, inverse-trigonometric calculus and polar area appear in every board's compulsory or core pure content.** Edexcel 9FM0, AQA 7367, OCR A H245 and OCR B (MEI) H645 also share the differential-equations block, from integrating factors through to coupled systems. The differences come in four groups. The first is where each board draws the AS line: OCR A teaches no series and no calculus at AS, while AQA teaches polar sketching, hyperbolic definitions and the Maclaurin standard series at AS. The second is option-only content: groups, number theory, recurrence relations, conics, t-formulae, Taylor and L'Hôpital, and partial differentiation. The third is Cambridge 9231, which makes eigenvalues, reduction formulae, arc length and rational-function graphs compulsory, but drops improper integrals, volumes, mean value and oscillation modelling, and assumes basic complex numbers from 9709. The fourth is Edexcel IAL, which spreads the same material over FP1–FP3 and lets students drop FP2 or FP3. Memorisation follows the same pattern. **Pearson, AQA and OCR (both specs) publish an essentially identical "must know" list**, but Cambridge's MF19 leaves out the polar area, the vector product, de Moivre, arc length and every vector-distance formula, so 9231 students must memorise far more. The recommended workbook has **40 units: 14 in Part A (AS Core Pure), 14 in Part B (A2 Core Pure) and 12 in Part C (Further Pure options)**. Every coded spec point in the research notes is placed in exactly one unit, except AQA's cross-cutting overarching themes.

## Six specifications share a DfE core but slice it into different papers

The four English specifications are built on the same Department for Education content, and the shared lists show it: MEI notes that its Core Pure "adds the vector product (Pv7, Pv8) to the DfE content" ([H645 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf)). What differs is how much of the qualification is pure and how the papers carve it up. **AQA makes compulsory pure two-thirds of the A level** (200 of 300 marks) and does not split content between its two pure papers ([7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)). **Edexcel, OCR A and MEI make the compulsory pure core exactly 50%.** Edexcel and OCR A can rise to 75% if a student takes the pure options, but MEI's Extra Pure is only a 16⅔% minor option ([9FM0 spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/a-level-l3-further-mathematics-specification.pdf); [H245 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf)). AQA has no pure option at all: Paper 3 is always two of mechanics, statistics and discrete ([7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)).

The AS specifications are strict subsets that reuse the A-level codes. **Edexcel 8FM0 Core Pure is exactly CP 1.1, 2.1–2.7, 3.1–3.8, 4.1–4.3, 5.1 and 6.1–6.5, worded identically to 9FM0** ([8FM0 spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/as-l3-further-mathematics-specification.pdf)). AQA's AS uses 41 of its 79 compulsory codes, and 10 of those 41 are narrower at AS. Sections I (differential equations) and J (numerical methods) are A level only ([7366 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)). OCR A's AS Y531 contains only proof, complex numbers without exponential form, all of matrices, four vector statements and roots of polynomials. There are **no series, hyperbolic functions, calculus, polar coordinates or differential equations at OCR A AS** ([H235 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308750-specification-accredited-as-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h235.pdf)). MEI's AS Y410 is the "(a)" sections of Y420. Unusually, it includes planes, three-plane arrangements and the angle between planes, but **no line equations and no vector product**, which come only in the "(b)" part ([H645 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf)).

The two international specifications are built differently. Cambridge 9231's pure content is 13 numbered topics: 1.1–1.7 in Paper 1 and 2.1–2.6 in Paper 2. Both papers assume 9709 Papers 1 and 3, which is why Paper 1 has no complex-number topic and Paper 2 starts directly at de Moivre. The 2028–2030 syllabus states "There are no significant changes which affect teaching" ([9231 2026–27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf); [9231 2028–30](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/744603-2028-2030-syllabus.pdf)). Edexcel IAL divides its material into three units. FP1 is AS-level: complex basics, quadratic roots, numerical root-finding, the parabola and rectangular hyperbola, 2×2 matrices, Σ series and induction. FP2 covers inequalities, the method of differences, de Moivre, loci and transformations, first- and second-order DEs, Maclaurin and Taylor series, and polar coordinates. FP3 covers hyperbolics, conics, inverse-function calculus, reduction formulae, arc length, vectors, 3×3 matrices and eigenvectors. IAL has **no groups, number theory, recurrence relations, Leibnitz, L'Hôpital, Simpson's rule, SHM, coupled systems or mean value** ([IAL spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/International%20Advanced%20Level/Mathematics/2018/Specification-and-Sample-Assessment/international-a-level-maths-spec.pdf)).

Three patterns matter most for a writer. First, **the same topic sits at different stages on different boards**. The method of differences is AS for AQA and MEI but A level for Edexcel and OCR A. The vector product is AS for OCR A, A level for AQA and MEI, and option content for Edexcel ([H235 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308750-specification-accredited-as-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h235.pdf); [7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)). AQA's AS stands out: it includes the Maclaurin standard series (D6) but not the general term (D5), and polar sketching (G1–G2) but not polar area (G3) ([7366 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)). Second, **AQA and Cambridge are the outliers in what they make compulsory**. AQA's compulsory content includes rational-function graphs and inequalities (D8–D16), eigenvalues and diagonalisation (C10–C11), reduction formulae, arc length (E7–E8) and numerical methods (J1–J3), all of which are options on Edexcel ([7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)). OCR A has no eigenvalue work anywhere, not even in Y545 ([H245 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf)). Cambridge Paper 2 adds two outcomes no other board has: implicit and parametric second derivatives (2.3) and rectangle bounds such as 1 + ln n > Σ1/r > ln(n+1) (2.4). Cambridge also leaves out improper integrals, volumes, mean value and SHM ([9231 2026–27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)). Third, **the two pure options share less than their names suggest**. OCR A Y545 and MEI Y435 overlap on recurrence relations, groups and partial differentiation. Only Y545 has number theory, the scalar triple product, Hessian classification, reduction formulae and arc length. Only Y435 has eigenvectors, Cayley–Hamilton and grad. Y435 also excludes second-order partial derivatives, which Y545 requires ([H645 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf); [H245 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf)).

Depth also differs inside rows that look the same. IAL requires students to **prove de Moivre for any integer n**. Cambridge requires a proof for positive integers. The English boards only require students to use it ([IAL spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/International%20Advanced%20Level/Mathematics/2018/Specification-and-Sample-Assessment/international-a-level-maths-spec.pdf); [9231 2026–27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)). Cambridge restricts eigenvalues to real and distinct values. Edexcel FP2 includes repeated and complex eigenvalues and orthogonal diagonalisation of symmetric matrices ([9FM0 spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/a-level-l3-further-mathematics-specification.pdf)). OCR A and MEI expect only cosh²x − sinh²x ≡ 1 as prior knowledge and exclude other identities. AQA (H6) and Cambridge (2.1) require the full set, including sech²x ≡ 1 − tanh²x and sinh 2x ([H245 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf); [7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)).

## Cambridge's formula list omits what English booklets print

The four English specifications publish essentially the same "must know without being given" list. Its pure items are: mod-arg and exponential multiplication and division; the meanings of |z−a| = r and arg(z−a) = θ; e^{iθ} = cos θ + i sin θ; the 2×2 determinant and inverse; "AB means B then A"; (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹; Σr; the root–coefficient relations for quadratics and cubics; the exponential definitions of cosh, sinh and tanh; volumes of revolution; ẍ = −ω²x; and the scalar product, the line r = a + tb and the plane (r−a)·n = 0. Pearson ([9FM0 Appendix 1](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/a-level-l3-further-mathematics-specification.pdf)), AQA ([7367 Appendix B](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)) and OCR ([H245 §5d](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf); [H645 §5d](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf)) all print it. One small difference: Pearson and OCR list derivatives and integrals of sinh kx and cosh kx as must-know, while AQA's calculus list does not mention them. AQA's rule spells out the principle all of these lists follow: such formulae "may only be provided where they are the starting point for a proof or as a result to be proved" ([7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF)). AS students get the same list, including items such as e^{iθ} that go beyond AS content ([7366 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)).

The booklets differ much more than the lists. **Cambridge 9231 is the heaviest memorisation load.** MF19 is a single list for 9709 and 9231, and "All formulae in the list may be needed for this syllabus" ([9231 2026–27](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)). Its Further Pure section prints only the three Σ results, the Maclaurin series, the t-results, three hyperbolic identities with the logarithmic forms, the derivatives of inverse and hyperbolic functions, and a short integral table ([MF19](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/417318-list-of-formulae-and-statistical-tables.pdf)). The syllabus itself tells students to *recall* the root–coefficient relations, singularity, the determinant as an area scale factor, polar area, the vector product and the meaning of CF and PI. Everything else must also be learned: matrix inverses and transformation matrices, de Moivre and zⁿ ± z⁻ⁿ, the three plane forms, point–plane and skew-line distances, arc length and surface area, the integrating factor, the PI trial forms, and the definitions and derivatives of sech, cosech and coth. The June 2023 examiner report confirms these gaps cost marks. On the point–plane formula, candidates "often omitted the constant term"; on polar area, "a few missed the ½" ([June 2023 ER mirror](https://pastpapers.co/cie/A-Level/Mathematics-Further-9231/2023-May-June/9231_s23_er.pdf)). In the other direction, **Cambridge is the only board that prints the t-formulae**, which Edexcel FP1 students must memorise.

## Forty units in three parts place every spec point once

The ordering follows four rules. First, prerequisites come first: complex numbers come before roots of polynomials, inverses before the three-planes problem, and the vector product before skew-line distance. Second, Part A holds content that is AS on most English boards or in Cambridge Paper 1 or IAL FP1. Topics whose stage differs by board are tagged. Third, a topic that is core for at least one board goes in Part A or B even where another board treats it as an option. This keeps the workbook free of duplicates: the vector product (A10) is core for OCR A, AQA, MEI, Cambridge and IAL FP3, so Edexcel FP1 students use the same unit. Likewise eigenvalues, reduction formulae, arc length and numerical methods (B12–B14) are core for AQA or Cambridge. Fourth, single-board topics sit at the end of their group so that other students can skip them. Board tags are **Ed** (9FM0 Core Pure "CP", options "FP1"/"FP2"; AS options "AS FP1"/"AS FP2"), **IAL**, **AQA**, **OCR** (OCR A), **MEI** and **CAIE**. A1, A5, A6, A9, A13, B1, B6, B13, B14, C9, C11 and C12 are lecture pairs, and the rest are single 20–30-minute lectures.

Read across by board, the route maps follow. An **Edexcel 8FM0** student takes A1–A9, A11, A12 and A14 for Core Pure (A4 only for 4.3, A14 only for 5.1). AS FP1 adds C1, C4, A10, B14 and A13. FP2 adds C6–C11, B12 and B13. An **IAL** student takes A1–A6, A8, B14 and C4 for FP1. FP2 adds A13, A4, B1–B3, B8–B10, C2, C3 and C6. The A level adds A10, A11, B1–B14 and C2 (D7). An **OCR A** AS student takes A1–A3 and A5–A11 (A9 only for lines, A10 only for 4.04g, A11 only for 4.03r). The A level adds A4, A12, A14 and B1–B11. Y535 adds A10 (8.04a–d) and C7–C12 at their AS points, and Y545 adds the rest of C7–C12, A10 (8.04e) and B13 (8.06). Two placements cross the A/B line, so a Cambridge AS student needs B8 (polar) in the AS year, and AQA AS students need B3, B4 and B8 in part. A11 (three planes) is in Part A because it is AS for Edexcel and MEI, although Cambridge examines it in Paper 2. Only two spec statements had to be split by sub-part: CAIE 2.5(3) (multiple angles, powers and series go to B1, roots of unity to B2) and IAL FP2 3.2 (de Moivre applications to B1, roots of a complex number to B2). **The only points not placed in any unit are AQA's overarching themes OT1.1–OT3.5** (argument and proof, problem solving, modelling). They are cross-cutting process statements rather than content, so each unit's exercises should carry them. MEI's withdrawn minor option Further Pure with Technology (Y436, final assessment Summer 2028) is outside the brief and is not mapped ([H645 spec v2.1](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The main design lesson is that **"AS Core Pure" is not one shared syllabus but four different cuts of the same content**, and the cuts disagree most on series, vectors and early calculus. A workbook that follows any one board's AS boundary would put OCR A's summation, MEI's line equations or AQA's polar sketching in the wrong year for someone. Ordering by prerequisite, and tagging each unit with its stage on each board, avoids that. It also lets one unit serve both a core student and an option student. A10 (vector product) and B12–B14 (eigenvectors, reduction formulae and arc length, numerical methods) show why this matters: these are options on Edexcel but compulsory on AQA or Cambridge. Writing them twice would waste effort and invite inconsistency.

The second lesson is that **the effective memorise list depends more on the booklet than on the official list**, which is almost identical across the English boards. Cambridge students have the shortest booklet and the most to learn: polar area, the vector product, distances, de Moivre and arc length. Edexcel FP1 students must memorise exactly the formulae that Cambridge and AQA print: the t-formulae, Simpson's rule and the numerical DE formulae. Each unit should therefore end with a board-tagged "given or learn" box built from the formula table above. Most worked examples should go into the units used by all six specifications (A3, A6, A8, B1, B4, B6 and B8). The units that serve only one or two boards (C1, C9, C10 and C12) can stay short.
