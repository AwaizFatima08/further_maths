# Twenty-four units cover six Further Statistics syllabuses

The six exam boards' Further Mathematics statistics components overlap much less than their shared titles suggest. **The χ² test for a contingency table is the only topic that every component examines as its own content.** Everything else is split along a few clear lines. Pearson Edexcel 9FM0 alone covers the negative binomial distribution, F-tests and χ² tests for a variance. Only Edexcel and Cambridge 9231 examine probability generating functions (PGFs). Only OCR A and Cambridge examine the full set of non-parametric tests (sign, Wilcoxon signed-rank and Wilcoxon rank-sum). AQA has no correlation, no regression and no goodness-of-fit test. MEI is the only board that examines software output, simulation and effect size. Edexcel IAL has **no S4 unit**: the IAL suite has only ever contained S1–S3, and S3 has no t-distribution. The boards' official "must memorise" lists are almost identical: Pearson, AQA and OCR each list only the data mean x̄ and the standardised variable Z = (X−μ)/σ. The formulae students really have to memorise are hidden in content statements that say "know", "prove" or "derive", and in what each booklet leaves out. A single workbook covering all boards therefore works best as 11 AS units and 13 A2 units. Each unit is tagged by board, and a route map per board picks out the units each student needs. The rest of this report sets out each board's content, compares the topics in one grid, lists what students must memorise, and gives the unit ordering with the official spec URLs.

## Six syllabuses share one label but differ in size and shape

The size of the statistics component varies more than threefold between boards, so "Further Statistics" alone tells you little about how much a student studies.

**Pearson Edexcel 9FM0** has two papers, each **1 h 30 min, 75 marks and 25% of the A level**. Further Statistics 1 (Paper 3B) has eight topics: discrete distributions, Poisson and binomial, geometric and negative binomial, hypothesis tests for Poisson and geometric parameters, the Central Limit Theorem, χ² tests, PGFs, and quality of tests. Further Statistics 2 (Paper 4B) has seven: regression, continuous distributions, correlation, combinations of normal variables, normal-based estimation, χ² and F tests for variance, and t-tests. **FS2 can only be taken together with FS1** ([9FM0 spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/a-level-l3-further-mathematics-specification.pdf)). The AS 8FM0 has cut-down versions of both. **AS FS1** is a **40-mark section of the 80-mark Paper 2**. It covers discrete distributions, Poisson and binomial, the Poisson hypothesis test, and χ² tests (goodness of fit to the discrete uniform, binomial and Poisson only). **AS FS2** keeps only regression, correlation, and continuous random variables with pdfs of the form kxⁿ ([8FM0 spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/as-l3-further-mathematics-specification.pdf); [8FM0/23 QP](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A-Level/Mathematics/2017/Exam-materials/8fm0-23-que-20220524.pdf)).

**Edexcel IAL has no S4.** Both the 2018 and the earlier 2013 IAL specifications list only S1 (WST01), S2 (WST02) and S3 (WST03). The IAL formulae booklet is titled "Statistics S1 – S3" ([IAL spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/International%20Advanced%20Level/Mathematics/2018/Specification-and-Sample-Assessment/international-a-level-maths-spec.pdf); [IAL 2013 spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/International%20Advanced%20Level/Mathematics/2013/Specification%20and%20sample%20assessments/9781446925201_IAL_Maths_Iss3.pdf)). The name "S4" almost certainly comes from the old UK Edexcel GCE (pre-2017). Its content (t, F, χ² for variance, paired t, power) now sits in 9FM0, and **no IAL unit examines it**. Each IAL unit is **1 h 30 min, 75 marks and 16⅔% of the IAL**. **S3 counts only towards Further Mathematics**, while S1 and S2 can count towards either Mathematics or Further Mathematics, but a unit result cannot be used for both. S3 covers five areas: linear combinations of normal variables; sampling methods; estimation, z-confidence intervals and z-tests for one and two samples (the spec says "A knowledge of the t-distribution is not required"); χ² goodness of fit and contingency tables, including fits to the normal and continuous uniform; and Spearman's rank with tests of zero correlation ([IAL spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/International%20Advanced%20Level/Mathematics/2018/Specification-and-Sample-Assessment/international-a-level-maths-spec.pdf)).

**AQA 7367** examines statistics as a **50-mark answer booklet inside the 2-hour, 100-mark Paper 3**, shared with a second option. Statistics is therefore about **one-sixth of the A level**. AS 7366 examines it as a **40-mark booklet inside Paper 2** (1 h 30 min, 80 marks) ([7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF); [7367/3S June 2022](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2022/june/AQA-73673S-QP-JUN22.PDF); [7366/2S June 2023](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2023/june/AQA-73662S-QP-JUN23.PDF)). The content has eight sections, SA–SH, with **36 numbered points**: discrete random variables, Poisson, Type I/II errors, continuous random variables, χ² tests for association, the exponential distribution, the one-sample t-test, and confidence intervals. The AS has 25 of these points. It drops the exponential and t-test sections entirely and leaves out power, the cdf, the rectangular distribution, Yates' correction and t-intervals ([7366 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7366-SP-2017.PDF)).

**OCR A Y542** is **75 marks in 1 h 30 min, 25% of H245**. It has nine sections, 5.01–5.09. The AS paper, Y532 (**60 marks, 75 min, 33⅓% of H235**), keeps the "Stage 1" content: combinatorial probability, discrete random variables (including the geometric), χ² tests, correlation and regression. The full A level adds continuous random variables (including the exponential and the cdf of Y = g(X)), linear combinations, z-tests and z-intervals using the CLT, further goodness of fit, and **sign, Wilcoxon signed-rank and rank-sum tests**. **No t-distribution appears anywhere in Y542** ([H245 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf); [H235 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308750-specification-accredited-as-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h235.pdf)).

**OCR B (MEI)** is built in layers. Its "(a)" content is identical across AS Y412, Statistics Minor Y432 and the first part of Statistics Major Y422. It covers sampling, discrete distributions, bivariate data and χ² tests. Y412 and Y432 are each **60 marks in 1 h 15 min**. The Major adds the "(b)" content: continuous random variables, inference with z and t, the single-sample Wilcoxon test, and spreadsheet simulation. It is **120 marks in 2 h 15 min and 33⅓% of H645**, the largest statistics component of any board ([H645 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf); [H635 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308764-specification-accredited-as-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h635.pdf)).

**Cambridge 9231 Paper 4** is **50 marks in 1 h 30 min, with 5–7 compulsory questions**. It is worth **40% of the AS (taken with Paper 1) or 20% of the A level**. It has exactly five sub-topics: 4.1 continuous random variables, 4.2 inference using normal and t-distributions, 4.3 χ² tests, 4.4 non-parametric tests, and 4.5 PGFs. This content is **identical in the 2023–25, 2026–27 and 2028–30 syllabuses** ([9231 2026–27 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf); [9231 2028–30 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/744603-2028-2030-syllabus.pdf)). Paper 4 assumes all of 9709 S1 and S2. Cambridge's prior-learning guide sorts the S2 content three ways. Poisson basics, continuous random variables, the CLT and test terminology are **required**. Linear combinations of normals and the z-interval and z-test are **recommended**. The Poisson approximations, the proportion CI and calculating Type I/II error probabilities are **not required** ([Guide to prior learning](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/676396-prior-learning-guidance.pdf)). Cambridge also allows **only a scientific calculator, with no graphical or CAS models** ([9231 2026–27 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)). Cambridge students therefore read tables far more than Edexcel, AQA or MEI students, who may take distribution probabilities and critical values from a calculator.

The grid shows three patterns that should shape the workbook. First, the **AS/A split is not consistent across boards**. The geometric distribution is AS at OCR and MEI but A level only at Edexcel. Continuous random variables are AS at Edexcel and AQA but A level at OCR and MEI. z-confidence intervals are AS at AQA but A level everywhere else. A unit's place in the workbook therefore has to follow the teaching logic, with board tags flagging where a board puts the topic at a different stage. Second, **boards choose different branches of inference**. Edexcel goes parametric and broad (t, F, χ² for variance). OCR A goes non-parametric and avoids t. Cambridge does both t and non-parametric tests. AQA does just a one-sample t. MEI uses t and a single Wilcoxon test, framed around software output. Third, small **rule differences matter in exams**. Yates' correction is required by OCR A, A level only at AQA, "not expected" at MEI, and excluded at IAL and Cambridge. The CLT rule of thumb is n > about 25 at OCR A but n > 30 at MEI ([H245 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf); [H645 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf)). Spearman's rank with ties can be set at Edexcel but is excluded at OCR and MEI.

## Booklets give the test statistics but students must still supply the procedures

Every board gives students a formulae booklet, but the booklets differ in their tables as much as in their formulae. That affects how a workbook should teach critical values.

**Edexcel's GCE booklet (X54458A)** is the most generous. It is one booklet for 8MA0, 9MA0, 8FM0 and 9FM0. Its Further Statistics section gives the PGF definitions and the mean and variance via PGFs. It has a table of the binomial, Poisson, geometric and negative binomial distributions, listing the pmf, mean, variance and PGF of each. It gives the continuous uniform mean and variance, Sxx/Sxy, r, b, RSS and Spearman's rs. It gives every sampling result: the z, χ² for variance, one-sample and paired t, F and pooled two-sample t statistics. The tables needed for A level FM are binomial cdf, Poisson cdf, χ², PMCC and Spearman critical values, random numbers, **t and F**, plus normal percentage points. The tables listed for AS FM leave out t and F ([GCE booklet](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/X_18pt_A4_Formulae_Booklet_and_Statistical_Tables.pdf)). **The IAL booklet** (Issue 2, January 2021) is arranged by unit. Its S3 pages give expectation algebra, the sampling distributions, Spearman's rs and the χ² statistic, with χ², correlation critical values and random-number tables. **It has no t or F tables** ([IAL booklet, third-party copy](https://media.publit.io/file/formularsheetas/alevelmaths/IAL-Mathematics-Formula-Book.pdf)).

**AQA's booklet** (v1.5, February 2018) gives the least. Its statistics content is basic probability, the standard deviation, the binomial and Poisson pmf, mean and variance, the sampling distributions (including the t statistic) and the contingency-table χ² statistic. It has just three tables: **t, χ² and PMCC critical values**. There are **no normal, binomial or Poisson tables**, because AQA requires a calculator that gives distribution probabilities ([AQA booklet](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/AQA-AS-A-FMATHS-FORMULAE.PDF)). **OCR A's H245 booklet** gives the distribution tables for the binomial, U(n), geometric and Poisson, and the pdf, mean and variance for the continuous uniform, **exponential** and normal. It gives Wilcoxon normal approximations, normal percentage points, r and rs critical values, χ², and **Wilcoxon T and W critical values**. It has **no t table**, which matches the spec ([H245 booklet](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308765-a-level-further-mathematics-a-formulae-booklet.pdf)). The AS H235 booklet stops at r, rs and χ² ([H235 booklet](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308766-as-level-further-mathematics-a-formulae-booklet.pdf)). **The MEI booklet** is unusual because it prints **tables of confidence-interval forms and hypothesis tests**. These include z and t intervals and the t-test for a mean. Its statistical tables are r, rs, χ², t, Normal and **Wilcoxon single-sample** critical values, with no rank-sum table ([MEI booklet](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/752530-formulae-booklet.pdf)). **Cambridge's MF19** gives the 9709 statistics block, including the geometric pmf and mean but **not its variance**. Its Further block has only two items: the pooled common-variance estimate and the PGF mean and variance formulae. Its tables are the normal distribution function, normal critical values, t, χ², and **Wilcoxon signed-rank and rank-sum** critical values with their normal approximations ([9231 2026–27 syllabus, MF19](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)).

The official memorisation appendices are identical and short. Pearson's 9FM0 Appendix 1, AQA's Appendix B and OCR's section 5d (for both FM A and MEI) list only **x̄ = Σx/n = Σfx/Σf and Z = (X−μ)/σ** for statistics ([9FM0 spec](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Mathematics/2017/specification-and-sample-assesment/a-level-l3-further-mathematics-specification.pdf); [7367 spec](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/mathematics/specifications/AQA-7367-SP-2017.PDF); [H245 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308752-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-a-h245.pdf); [H645 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf)). Inferred: the conditions for Poisson and geometric models, the standard error σ/√n, and residual = observed − predicted ([H645 spec](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308768-specification-accredited-a-level-gce-further-mathematics-b-mei-h645.pdf)) |
| Cambridge 9231 P4 | Inferred from MF19 gaps: the two-sample t statistic (x̄₁ − x̄₂)/√(s²(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)) with n₁ + n₂ − 2 degrees of freedom; the paired t statistic; the unpooled normal statistic using √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂); all confidence-interval forms; the χ² statistic and its degrees-of-freedom rules; the PGFs of the discrete uniform, binomial, geometric and Poisson; cdf definitions; and the geometric variance (which can be derived from the PGF). The pooled-variance formula is printed only in deviation form, so students must convert from Σx and Σx² themselves ([9231 2026–27 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697357-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf)) |

Cambridge's examiner reports show that these gaps are where marks are lost. The June 2023 report records candidates who used z instead of t, pooled variances without justification, wrote a cdf as "0 otherwise" instead of setting F = 1 above the range, added PGFs instead of multiplying them, and could not state that Wilcoxon tests assume a symmetric distribution ([9231 June 2023 examiner report](https://pastpapers.co/cie/A-Level/Mathematics-Further-9231/2023-May-June/9231_s23_er.pdf)). The workbook's memorise boxes should be built around choosing the right test, not just recalling formulae.

## Eleven AS units and thirteen A2 units serve every board

The ordering below follows four principles. Discrete distributions come before the inference that uses them. Continuous random variables close Part 1 so that Part 2 can start straight on cdfs. z-inference comes before power, t and non-parametric tests, so that each of these can be taught as a contrast with z. Content that only one board examines comes at the end of its natural group, so that other students can skip it without breaking the sequence. Each unit is tagged with board codes: **Ed** (9FM0/8FM0), **IAL**, **AQA**, **OCR-A**, **MEI** and **CAIE**. ## Conclusion

The most important result for the workbook is that **"AS" and "A2" are teaching stages, not shared content sets**. A workbook that copies any one board's AS/A split will put Edexcel's geometric distribution, AQA's confidence intervals and OCR's continuous random variables in the wrong part for other boards. A sequence based on dependencies, with board tags and per-board route maps, avoids this. The comparison also shows where effort should go. Units 7 (χ² tests) and 10–12 (continuous random variables) serve five or six specifications each, so they deserve the most worked examples. Units 14, 21 and 24 each serve a single board and can be short.

The second result is that **the hard part of memorisation is choosing the right procedure, not recalling formulae**. Every booklet prints the test statistics, but none tells students when to pool variances, when t replaces z, when Yates' correction applies, or when Wilcoxon's symmetry assumption is needed. These are the errors Cambridge's examiners report most often. Table use is also not uniform. Cambridge students have only scientific calculators and must read Φ, t, χ² and Wilcoxon tables, while AQA students get no normal table at all. Each unit should therefore include table-reading practice tagged by board, alongside "which test?" decision exercises.
